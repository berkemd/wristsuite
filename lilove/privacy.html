LiLove — Privacy Policy

Last updated 16 August 2026 · English · Türkçe below

English

The one sentence that matters: your habit titles, your note text and your task names never leave your device. The coach receives numbers.

Most privacy policies say "we take your privacy seriously" and then describe collecting everything. This one describes an architecture instead, so you can check it against what the app does.

Where your content lives

Habits, goals, tasks, notes, achievements, avatar and statistics are stored on your device. Of the app's endpoints, the overwhelming majority never touch a network at all — they read and write local storage. Deleting the app deletes that data. There is no account on our side holding a copy, because there is no account.

What is sent to the coach, exactly

LiLove's coach is the one feature that requires a connection. When you ask for a daily insight, a performance summary, a suggestion, or you ask the coach a question about your own tracked data, the app builds a numeric summary and sends that.

Streak lengths · completion rates · counts of habits and goals · the hour of day at which items tend to be completed · how many days of history exist Habit titles · goal titles · task names · note text · your name · your email · your contacts · your location · any device or advertising identifier

This is a design decision with a stated reason, and the reason is written into the code: nearly everything a coach can usefully say about consistency already follows from the numbers. Sending the text as well would spend your privacy without buying anything back.

The coach's instructions also say, in the model's own prompt, that it has never seen your words and must not pretend to know what a habit is about; that when the data is too thin to support a claim it must say so rather than guess; and that it must not give medical, psychological or nutritional advice, must not diagnose, and must not mention weight, calories or body image.

The one identifier that is sent, and why

Alongside the numeric summary, the app sends the subscription transaction identifier Apple issues for your purchase. It is used for two things: to check that the request comes from an active subscription, and as the key for a per-subscription rate limit. It is deliberately not an IP-based limit, because rate-limiting by IP makes users behind the same network cut each other off. This identifier is not a device identifier and not an advertising identifier, it is not linked to your name or email — we do not have those — and it is not retained after the request.

How the request is handled

The numeric summary is sent over HTTPS to a small relay operated for LiLove, running on Cloudflare Workers, which calls Cloudflare's own Workers AI to produce the answer and returns it. The relay writes no log of your summaries, stores no answers, and has no database or key-value store attached to it; responses are marked no-store. If the model's reply does not match the expected structure, the app reports that the coach could not answer — it never substitutes a sentence we wrote and presents it as the coach's.

Purchases

Subscriptions and coin packs are sold by Apple. Apple tells the app whether an entitlement is active; it does not give us your payment details, and we never see your card, your Apple ID or your billing address. Purchase validation is handled through the subscription infrastructure Apple provides. Refunds are Apple's to grant, through the same channel as any App Store purchase.

What we do not do

No advertising. No advertising identifier. No third-party analytics SDK. No selling or sharing of data with anyone for their own purposes. No profile is built about you across apps or across time. No email list is created from your use of the app.

Legal basis for anything that happens

  • Everything stored on your device — we are not processing it, because it never reaches us. It sits in your device, under your control, and is gone when you delete the app. There is no legal basis for us to state, because there is no processing by us.
  • The numeric summary sent when you use the coach — performance of the contract you entered into when you asked for the feature, GDPR Art. 6(1)(b), and the equivalent under KVKK Art. 5(2)(c). It is sent only when you actively request a coach response; nothing is sent in the background.
  • Purchases — performance of contract, and Apple's own terms govern the payment itself.

Your rights

Under GDPR and KVKK you may ask what is held about you, ask for a copy, ask for correction or erasure, object to processing, and withdraw consent. In LiLove's case the honest answer to most of these is short: we hold nothing about you to give you, correct or erase, because your content stays on your device and the numeric summaries are not retained. You can exercise the strongest version of erasure yourself at any time by deleting the app.

Write to brkekahraman@icloud.com and you will get an answer from a person.

Complaining about us

If you are in the EU or UK you may complain to your national data protection authority. In Türkiye you may apply to the Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurumu (KVKK). You do not have to contact us first, though it is usually faster.

How long anything is kept

On your device: until you delete it or delete the app. On our side: the numeric summary exists only for the duration of the request needed to answer it and is not written to a log or a database.

International transfers

The relay and the language-model provider that answers coach requests may process the numeric summary outside your country, including in the United States. The summary contains no titles, no note text and no identifiers, which is the point of sending numbers in the first place.

Children

LiLove is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect anything from them. Because the app holds your content on your device and asks for no account, no name and no email, there is nothing about a child for us to have.

Changes to this policy

If what the app does changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. Material changes will also be described in the app's release notes rather than buried here.
