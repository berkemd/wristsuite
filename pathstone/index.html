Pathstone

One small thing, every day.

Pathstone gives your child one small thing to do each day — not on the screen, but in the world. It takes a few minutes, then the app closes.

The choice is not random. Pathstone follows nine capabilities and picks the one that has been practised least. Difficulty rises only after the last few went well, and drops the moment your child says a task was hard.

The nine capabilities

  • Spotting mistakes
  • Finding another way
  • Looking at your own work
  • Finishing
  • Working together
  • Respect
  • Order
  • Learning how to learn
  • Finding what interests you

What a task looks like

“Find something you started and did not finish. Finish it today.”

“Listen to someone without interrupting. All the way to the end.”

“Ask a grown-up what they liked doing most when they were your age. Then ask one more question.”

“Improve something by taking a part away instead of adding one.”

What grown-ups see

Behind a parental gate: how many stones have been placed, which capabilities have been practised, and exactly why today’s task was chosen — the same numbers the app itself used to choose it.

What Pathstone does not do

No streaks

A streak counter moves the reason to keep going away from the work and onto the fear of losing something. A child who misses a day has not ruined anything.

No badges, no confetti, no notifications

All of those exist to pull someone back to a screen. This app promises the opposite.

No account, no server, no analytics

There is no Pathstone server. Nothing leaves the device. See the privacy policy.

No date of birth

A grown-up sets the age band once, behind the parental gate.
