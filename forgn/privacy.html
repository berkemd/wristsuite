Privacy Policy / Gizlilik Politikası

Last updated / Son güncelleme: 6 September 2026 · 6 Eylül 2026
English first, Türkçesi aşağıda.

Your private workspace stays on your device. Your archive, projects and plans are stored in the app on your device. They are not uploaded to a developer-operated backend. User-initiated searches, web clipping and optional exports are described below.

There is no account, no sign-up, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no server operated by us.

You choose what leaves: literature searches and web clipping contact the selected public service. Version 1.3 adds previewed progress cards and recovery files sent only to the destination you select in the system share sheet. These additions become available after App Store approval.

1. Who we are

Forgn is developed by Berke Kahraman (Istanbul, Türkiye), the data controller for the purposes of the GDPR and the KVKK. Contact: brkekahraman@icloud.com.

2. What stays on your device

Forgn stores the following in the app's private storage on your iPhone:

  • Archive entries: your notes, clipped text, scanned text, dictated text, tags.
  • Projects, plan steps, the status you mark and the notes you attach to a step.
  • Suggestions you saved, hid, or turned into a project.
  • In 1.3, selected research sources, recorded queries and search dates, notebook notes and your optional public takeaway.
  • Your app settings, including whether Face ID unlock is on.

There is no automatic upload to us. Deleting the app removes its local data. Device backups and restoration, if enabled in your Apple settings, are managed by Apple. We hold no server copy. In 1.3, save an Export recovery copy from Profile before removing the app if you need an independent copy.

3. What leaves your device, and only when you ask

The following user actions contact external services. Your device communicates with the destination directly; Forgn does not route these requests through a developer-operated backend. Apple may also receive requests needed to check or restore your subscription.

When What is sent To whom
You run a prior-art / literature search The search words in the box, and your device's IP address (unavoidable in any internet request) Crossref and arXiv
You save a web page by address A normal web request for the address you entered, and your IP address The website you chose
You tap a patent database link The search words in the URL and the normal information sent by your browser, including your IP address Google Patents, Espacenet, or Lens
You share a progress card (1.3) The previewed image: your optional public takeaway and counts of saved sources and searches. Private project titles, source lists, queries and notes are excluded. The app or destination you choose in the system share sheet
You export a recovery copy (1.3) A JSON file containing your archive, projects, research notebooks and original recovery data. Purchase state and credentials are excluded. The location or app you choose in the system share sheet

Your full archive is not included in literature or website requests. A recovery export intentionally includes your full stored work, so keep it private. Search services and sharing destinations have their own privacy policies. Forgn does not log queries on a developer-operated server. Temporary card and export files are removed from the app cache after the share sheet closes.

4. Camera, photos and dictation

Forgn asks for no camera permission and no photo library permission, because it needs neither. Scanning a printed page uses your iPhone's built-in text recognition from the keyboard: iOS opens the camera, recognises the text on the device, and types the result into the app. Forgn never receives an image and no photo is saved.

Voice notes use the iOS dictation key on the keyboard. Speech handling is Apple's; see Apple's privacy documentation for how dictation works on your device.

5. Face ID

If you turn on Face ID unlock, iOS performs the check and tells the app only "succeeded" or "failed". Your biometric data never leaves the Secure Enclave and is never available to Forgn.

6. Purchases

Subscriptions are processed by Apple through the App Store. Forgn receives only a verified receipt of what was purchased and until when. We do not see, receive or store your name, email, card, or any payment detail. Manage or cancel a subscription in iPhone Settings → your name → Subscriptions.

External research services handle requests under their own policies linked above. arXiv retains IP addresses and request paths in logs for abuse protection and may use request information for research in the public interest. Although arXiv states that it does not attempt to identify browsing users, it treats this information as personal data. Forgn therefore declares Search History and Other Data (network request information) as linked data used for App Functionality and Other Purposes. Neither is used for advertising tracking. Forgn sends no account or advertising identifier with searches. Private project notes are not included in a search request.

7. Children

Forgn is a tool for research and invention and is not directed at children. We do not request a child’s profile or use advertising or analytics. Optional online services process requests as described above.

8. Your rights

Under the GDPR, the KVKK and similar laws you have rights of access, correction, deletion, portability and objection. For the workspace stored on your device:

  • Access and portability: In 1.3, Profile → Your data → Export recovery copy produces a JSON recovery file.
  • Correction: edit any entry in the app.
  • Deletion: delete an entry, or delete the app to remove everything.

We do not receive your in-app archive. If you contact support by email, we receive the address and content you send and use them to handle your request. Avoid sending sensitive project details unless necessary. Contact us about access or deletion of support correspondence.

9. Changes

If a future version of Forgn ever sends something new, this page will be updated before that version ships, and the change will be described in the App Store release notes. The date at the top always reflects the current text.
