Stemmata

Know what you may mine.

Text-and-data-mining permission is not a footnote. Across 350 live records measured from OpenAlex, only a fifth could be processed in full. Most tools hide this. Stemmata puts it on the card, with the reason written out: which licence, which publisher, which clause.

What it does

  • Permission on every work. Full text, metadata only, or closed — each with the reason that produced it. When the reservation set behind a decision is older than 180 days, the card says so instead of pretending the answer is fresh.
  • Search that runs offline and needs no model. A lexical index and a character-trigram vector work together; on inflected queries the vector leg raised recall from 7.4% to 44.1%.
  • Notes that merge without conflict. Edit the same note on two devices, offline, and both edits survive.
  • Export that respects the answer. BibTeX, RIS and Markdown. A work marked closed does not leave in an export — eleven tests hold that line.
  • Lineage. References and citations drawn as a tree, so you can see where an argument came from.

Where your data is

On your device. There is no Stemmata server, no account and no telemetry. The app makes network requests in one place only — when you import a record from a public catalogue — and that request carries your search terms and nothing about you.

Stemmata reports the permission a publisher has declared. It is a reading of published licence and opt-out data, not legal advice.
